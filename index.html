“Ragtime” Cases the Landmark It Almost Blew Up

I read this short piece in the New Yorker today— please see the attachment above. I do not know why, after reading a brief summary of the play, “Ragtime,” that what seems like critical background research for into their characters, the actors in the new staging of the play had never visited the real Morgan Library. This sent me down a small rabbit hole researching various historical characters in the play, particularly, Evelyn Nesbit. The story of Harry Thaw, Stanford White, and Evelyn Nesbitt reads like an early version of the Jeffery Epstein files. This lead to another timely story printed in the New York Times in 1909 about the lifting of a tariff placed on artwork purchased in Europe that ultimately led to great works ending up in the collection of the Morgan Library, the MET and the Frick collection—the other attachments below were part off this journey of discovery this morning if you are interested. I hope you all are staying warm. Myra and I send our love as always, .M&.M.
